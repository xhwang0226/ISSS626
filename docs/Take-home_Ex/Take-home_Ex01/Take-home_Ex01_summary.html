1. Why this matters

The problem

  • Thailand had about 18,218 road deaths in 2021, a rate of 25.4 per 100,000, among the highest in Asia (WHO, 2023)
  • The Bangkok Metropolitan Region (BMR) is Thailand’s largest urban area
  • A concentration of accidents is not the same as higher risk: it may reflect traffic volume, road density or how accidents are recorded

Research questions

  1. Where and when are recorded road accidents in BMR concentrated in 2022?
  2. Along the road network, are accidents clustered, regular or random, compared with random placement on the network?
  3. Do conclusions change between planar and network views?

2. Study area and data

Study area: Bangkok + 5 vicinity provinces (Nonthaburi, Pathum Thani, Samut Prakan, Samut Sakhon, Nakhon Pathom), from geoBoundaries ADM1

4. Analytical approach

Accidents can only occur on roads, so the road network is the study space.

Question Main method Reference / null model Comparison
When? Monthly and daily counts – Holiday campaign periods
Where? (first-order) Network KDE (quartic kernel, 1 km along roads) – Planar KDE; NKDE at 0.5 and 2 km
Stable over time? Spatio-temporal KDE by day of year – STKDE by month
Clustered? (second-order) Network K- and G-functions, 50 simulations, Bangkok CSR on the road network Clark-Evans, G and L vs planar CSR


Key assumptions: events are located on the major road network; network CSR treats every road as equally likely, ignoring traffic volume; recorded accidents represent all accidents (they do not, see slide 3).

5. When do accidents happen?

  • Monthly counts range from about 210 (February) to 395 (October), higher in the second half of 2022
  • The largest daily peaks coincide with the New Year and Songkran campaign periods, up to about 36 accidents a day
  • Counts are not adjusted for traffic volume, so peaks mean more recorded accidents, not necessarily higher risk

6. Where do accidents concentrate on the network?

Accidents concentrate on a few specific roads

  • Corridors across southern Bangkok into Samut Prakan (highest density)
  • A north–south corridor into Pathum Thani
  • A south-western corridor towards Samut Sakhon

Most of the major road network, including much of inner Bangkok, has zero or near-zero density.

Planar KDE shows the same corridors but spreads them into areas without roads; NKDE pinpoints the road segments.

7. Are hotspots stable over the year?

Spatio-temporal KDE (bootstrap bandwidths: 3.1 km, 76 days)

  • Stable core: the hotspot in south-eastern Bangkok near Samut Prakan, and the corridor into Pathum Thani, persist all year
  • Changing periphery: the corridor towards Samut Sakhon is faint in February and April, but prominent by July and October

The 76-day bandwidth shows seasonal change; short holiday peaks are smoothed out (see slide 5).

9. Limitations

Data

  • Observation process: only national road agencies’ records; city streets are likely missing
  • No exposure data: without traffic volumes, concentration ≠ risk
  • Location quality: some accidents share exact locations, possibly fixed reference points
  • Single year (2022) and a 2026 road network for 2022 accidents

Methods

  • Results depend on bandwidth (tested at several values for planar KDE and NKDE)
  • Network CSR assumes equal exposure on every road
  • Network K-function covers Bangkok only, with 50 simulations, for computational reasons
  • The day-of-year STKDE smooths out short holiday peaks

10. Public-safety implications

Supported directly by the analysis

  • Target road segments, not whole provinces: focus audits, enforcement and engineering reviews on the high-density corridors
  • Investigate repeated locations at site level (junction design, signage, lighting)
  • Time campaigns for the New Year and Songkran periods and the second half of the year
  • Monitor the emerging Samut Sakhon corridor
  • Emergency response: check rescue-unit coverage along the high-density corridors

Requires additional information

  • Traffic volumes, to turn counts into risk per vehicle-kilometre
  • Cause, vehicle and road-design data, before designing specific interventions
  • City-street records from the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration
  • Multi-year data, to confirm that the patterns persist and to evaluate campaigns

Note

These findings describe where recorded accidents concentrate; they do not show why they occur.
